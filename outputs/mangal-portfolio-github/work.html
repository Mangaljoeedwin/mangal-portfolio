Positioning & Messaging · Strategic positioning

Building an AI-native CLM narrative

15–20%competitive win-rate improvement
+
01 · CONTEXT

What was happening?

SpotDraft had multiple AI capabilities, but the market story risked becoming a list of features in a category crowded with similar claims.

02 · CHALLENGE

What needed solving?

Create a differentiated narrative that made the product’s intelligence tangible, credible and commercially useful.

03 · INSIGHT

What shaped the approach?

Legal teams were not buying AI. They were buying speed, confidence and relief from repetitive contract work, without giving up control.

04 · STRATEGIC DECISION

The choice that focused the work

Position the portfolio around an AI-native way of working, connect every capability to a high-value legal job and make human control part of the promise.

05 · EXECUTION

From decision to market

01Portfolio positioning and narrative architecture02Persona-specific messaging for General Counsel and Legal Ops03Competitive differentiation and proof points04Sales story, pitch narrative and enablement
06 · IMPACT

What changed

The narrative improved clarity across campaigns and sales conversations, contributing to a 15–20% improvement in competitive win rates.

07 · LEARNING

What I would apply again

“A credible AI story starts with the workflow that changes, not the model behind it.”

Launching Sidebar: From AI capability to customer adoption

100+workspaces activated in 90 days
+
01 · CONTEXT

What was happening?

Sidebar introduced an AI assistant directly into the contract workflow. Success required more than a launch moment. Users needed to understand when and why to use it.

02 · CHALLENGE

What needed solving?

Build demand, equip customer-facing teams and turn early access into repeat usage.

03 · INSIGHT

What shaped the approach?

The strongest adoption trigger was not a broad promise of productivity. It was showing users the specific moments where Sidebar removed a frustrating step.

04 · STRATEGIC DECISION

The choice that focused the work

Run a staged beta-to-GA motion that treated activation and adoption as part of launch strategy.

05 · EXECUTION

From decision to market

01Beta recruitment and feedback program02Value proposition and launch brief03Sales and CS enablement04Email, in-app and first-use adoption journeys
06 · IMPACT

What changed

More than 100 workspaces adopted Sidebar in its first 90 days, with the launch contributing to expansion revenue and a broader $4M+ launch portfolio pipeline.

07 · LEARNING

What I would apply again

“For AI products, the first successful prompt is part of the GTM experience.”
Product Launches · PMM operating system

Building a scalable product launch engine

6+major launches orchestrated
+
01 · CONTEXT

What was happening?

A growing product roadmap meant releases varied widely in strategic importance, audience and readiness. A single launch playbook could not serve every release.

02 · CHALLENGE

What needed solving?

Create enough consistency to align teams without adding process for its own sake.

03 · INSIGHT

What shaped the approach?

The bottleneck was not asset production. It was early agreement on launch stakes, audience, evidence and ownership.

04 · STRATEGIC DECISION

The choice that focused the work

Introduce launch tiers, shared briefs, release calendars and channel plans that scaled effort to commercial importance.

05 · EXECUTION

From decision to market

01Tier 1, 2 and 3 launch model02Cross-functional launch brief and RACI03Beta-to-GA planning templates04Central release calendar and retrospectives
06 · IMPACT

What changed

The system supported 6+ major launches including Sidebar, Contract Analytics, Legal Hub, Smart Data Capture, Clickthrough and Microsoft Word integrations.

07 · LEARNING

What I would apply again

“A launch system creates speed by forcing the right decisions early.”
Product Adoption · Lifecycle growth

Driving product adoption through lifecycle marketing

25–30%increase in feature adoption
+
01 · CONTEXT

What was happening?

Customers could buy product value long before every user discovered or adopted it. Product communication was episodic rather than journey-led.

02 · CHALLENGE

What needed solving?

Connect onboarding, activation, feature discovery and expansion into measurable customer journeys.

03 · INSIGHT

What shaped the approach?

Users needed different proof and guidance based on role, product maturity and behavior. One newsletter could not do the work of lifecycle marketing.

04 · STRATEGIC DECISION

The choice that focused the work

Build segmented journeys around moments of value, combining behavioral signals with email and in-product guidance.

05 · EXECUTION

From decision to market

01Journey maps and activation definitions02Behavior-based segmentation03Customer.io lifecycle campaigns04Chameleon in-app guidance and adoption reporting
06 · IMPACT

What changed

Lifecycle and GTM programs increased feature adoption by approximately 25–30% and reduced time-to-value by around 20%.

07 · LEARNING

What I would apply again

“Adoption is where product promises become customer evidence.”
